How do I record a day?

Open Apex, tap Start a session, name it, and tap Start recording. Allow location when asked, and choose Precise — without precise location, speed and route are unreliable. Then put the phone in a zipped pocket. Recording continues with the screen locked.

Does it keep recording when my phone is in my pocket?

Yes. Apex uses background location, and iOS shows the blue location indicator while a session is running. Force-quitting Apex from the app switcher stops recording, and iOS may stop it if your battery dies. If a session is interrupted, Apex offers it back as a paused recording when you reopen the app — it never invents the time it missed.

My vertical or speed looks wrong.

Phone GPS is not a survey instrument. Apex discards poor fixes, requires a second reading to support a top speed, and uses an altitude deadband so noise does not pile up as vertical, but trees, gondolas, weather, and pocket position all affect it. If Apex labelled a lift as a run or a break as a queue, open the session, go to Lifts, tap the segment and correct it — the totals recalculate.

Are the lift wait times real resort wait times?

No. Apex has no resort feed of any kind. Every queue and lift figure is a timing of your own recording, on the day you recorded it. It cannot tell you how long a line is right now.

How accurate is jump detection?

It is experimental and has not been validated on snow. Apex looks for a drop in acceleration followed by an impact while you are moving downhill, and every detection has to be reviewed by you before it counts. Estimated distance is approach speed multiplied by estimated airtime, not a measurement of the takeoff-to-landing distance. Landing acceleration is shown as a raw sensor reading — Apex deliberately does not give a landing smoothness score, because nothing has been calibrated to support one. Expect both missed jumps and false detections, especially in moguls or on rough snow.

What is the run pattern score?

A heuristic about consistency and flow: 70% steadiness of speed and 30% regularity of GPS direction changes. A straight, steady groomer can score higher than a demanding technical run. It is not a measure of skill, control, or safe riding, and it is not a safety rating.

What does it do to my battery?

Continuous GPS, and the accelerometer when park detection is on, use meaningfully more battery than a phone sitting idle. Carry a charger for a full day. If you only want route and vertical, turn off park detection in Settings to reduce sensor use. Full-day battery use has not yet been measured on a physical iPhone.

Where is my data, and how do I back it up?

Everything stays on your iPhone; there is no account and no server. Go to More > Settings & data > Prepare JSON backup to write a backup file you can save or send wherever you like. Import merges anything missing without overwriting what you already have. Backups contain your recorded routes, so keep them somewhere private. Attached videos are not included in a JSON backup.

What is the demo session?

Illustrative sample data, labelled as a demo everywhere it appears, so you can see what the app looks like before you ride. It is excluded from your personal records and season totals, and you can remove it in Settings & data.

Apex says my saved data could not be opened.

Apex will not overwrite a data file it cannot read. Export a backup from Settings first — that exports the original file untouched — then email it to us and we will help you recover what is in it.

Is there an Apple Watch app, or trail navigation?

No. Apex is iPhone-only, and it has no trail map, no open/closed status, no route recommendations, and no automatic trick recognition.
